# 【S-008】Starryear — Visual Mark｜影像成标

Create one finished vertical 2:3 identity artwork from 2–5 photographs. Photography is the visual protagonist; the compact English art-wordmark and Brand Symbol act as a restrained identity signature. All elements must behave as one authored visual system rather than three stacked sections. The method stays fixed; composition, crop logic, geometry, palette, lettering, and symbol change with every source set.

## Workflow

1. Inspect the photographs as one collection. Internally select 3–7 decisive source facts across silhouette, contour, direction, negative space, structure, rhythm, light, and color.
2. Build a source-faithful photographic field in roughly 65–72% of the canvas. Avoid an equal-column contact sheet. Establish a dominant image and use asymmetric crops, scale contrast, slits, contour masks, controlled overlap, or negative space to integrate the remaining photographs.
3. Reserve roughly 28–35% of the canvas for a quiet identity area, including whitespace and the wordmark. Within it, extract and simplify the selected facts into one compact Brand Symbol: its bounding box should be about 22–32% of canvas width and 12–18% of canvas height. These dimensions describe the mark itself, not its surrounding area; do not enlarge it to fill the lower area. Merge, subtract, interlock, offset, or repeat only where the relationship remains traceable to the photographs. Favor one strong non-generic silhouette, intentional counterforms, and small-size legibility.
4. Add exactly one concise English name as an artistic wordmark. Use user wording when supplied; otherwise derive a source-grounded 1–3 word name. Design it as bespoke display lettering—not a caption—and keep every letter correct and legible. Keep the wordmark subordinate to photography, normally no wider than 40% of the canvas and no taller than 8%; avoid giant lettering or long swashes that turn it into another dominant image. Do not add Chinese or secondary text.
5. Create at least one meaningful visual relay across zones: a source contour, directional line, crop edge, negative-space channel, color accent, or symbol stroke should pass through or align with the wordmark and connect photography to the Brand Symbol. It must feel inevitable, not decorative.
6. Choose the Logo’s 1–2 main fill colors directly from visible regions of the supplied photographs. Internally identify the source region for each chosen color; retain its recognizable hue and saturation, with only small value adjustments for contrast. Any accent must also come from the photographs. Do not substitute a preset black, gold, olive, or other branding palette unless those colors are present in this source set. Derive the remaining layout colors from the same source palette; a quiet neutral background is allowed but must not replace the Logo’s source-derived main colors. Keep edges flat and print-friendly, with only a subtle unified tactile finish.
7. Return exactly one clean final artwork at 2:3. Do not return analysis, a contact sheet, sketches, alternate marks, mockups, or prompt explanation.

## Creative Standard

- Seek one clear “aha” relationship that makes the source photos and symbol suddenly read as the same visual idea.
- Create tension between contrasting source qualities: organic mass versus rigid rhythm, softness versus enclosure, or curved motion versus structural cuts.
- Use asymmetry and hierarchy. Do not distribute photographs equally merely for completeness.
- Typography may use custom high-contrast serif, italic, ligature, sliced counterform, elongated stroke, or controlled swash when it reinforces source geometry. Avoid generic centered subtitles and overdecorated script.
- Photography remains the largest visual event. The Brand Symbol is one small but readable scalable mark, surrounded by breathing room; never a half-page emblem, illustration, or cluster of mini-icons.

## Guardrails

- Preserve recognizable photographic truth. Cropping, masking, scale changes, light grading, and mild perspective correction are allowed; synthesized substitutes, duplicated subjects, and redrawn photo content are not.
- Every important lower-zone component must trace to visible evidence in at least one input photograph.
- Never default to circles, seals, badges, suns, mountains, camera apertures, location pins, shields, hearts, infinity signs, globes, leaves, crowns, or letter monograms.
- Do not use equal photo tiles, thick dividers, scrapbook devices, logo grids, stationery, packaging, signs, or app-icon mockups.
- The only permitted text is the chosen English wordmark. Exclude Chinese, captions, dates, signatures, watermarks, fabricated company claims, and pseudo-text.
- Avoid gradients, glow, beveling, chrome, liquid 3D, heavy shadows, random grain, malformed letters, generation seams, and illegible micro-detail.
